## 2. Information-Theoretic Perfect Secrecy (Shannon Proof)

### Theorem 1 (Shannon Perfect Secrecy for $k-1$ Shares)
Let $S \in \mathbb{F}_q$ be the secret master key. Let $f(x) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x]$ be a randomly chosen polynomial of degree at most $k-1$:
$$f(x) = S + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \dots + a_{k-1} x^{k-1} \pmod q$$
where $a_1, \dots, a_{k-1} \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{F}_q$ are chosen uniformly and independently at random.

For any subset of $m < k$ distinct evaluated shares $\{(x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2), \dots, (x_m, y_m)\}$ and any candidate secret $S' \in \mathbb{F}_q$:
$$P(S = S' \mid (x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_m, y_m)) = P(S = S') = \frac{1}{q}$$

#### Proof:
Fix any $m = k - 1$ points $(x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_{k-1}, y_{k-1})$ with pairwise distinct $x_i \in \mathbb{F}_q^*$. For any arbitrary choice of candidate secret $S' \in \mathbb{F}_q$, consider the system of $k$ linear equations in $k$ unknowns $(S', a_1, \dots, a_{k-1})$:
$$\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\
1 & x_1 & x_1^2 & \dots & x_1^{k-1} \\
1 & x_2 & x_2^2 & \dots & x_2^{k-1} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
1 & x_{k-1} & x_{k-1}^2 & \dots & x_{k-1}^{k-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
S' \\ a_1 \\ a_2 \\ \vdots \\ a_{k-1}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
S' \\ y_1 \\ y_2 \\ \vdots \\ y_{k-1}
\end{pmatrix} \pmod q$$

The coefficient matrix is a standard Vandermonde-type matrix. Since $0, x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{k-1}$ are all distinct elements in $\mathbb{F}_q$, its determinant is:
$$\det(V) = \prod_{0 \le i < j \le k-1} (x_j - x_i) \not\equiv 0 \pmod q$$

Thus, the system has a **unique** solution $(a_1, \dots, a_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{F}_q^{k-1}$ for **every single** $S' \in \mathbb{F}_q$. Because the coefficients $a_i$ were chosen uniformly at random from $\mathbb{F}_q^{k-1}$, each polynomial occurs with probability $q^{-(k-1)}$. Therefore:
$$P(S = S' \mid \text{Shares}_{k-1}) = \frac{q^{-(k-1)}}{\sum_{s \in \mathbb{F}_q} q^{-(k-1)}} = \frac{q^{-(k-1)}}{q \cdot q^{-(k-1)}} = \frac{1}{q}$$

Consequently:
$$H(S \mid \text{Shares}_{<k}) = H(S) \approx 255 \text{ bits}$$
The mutual information $I(S; \text{Shares}_{<k}) = H(S) - H(S \mid \text{Shares}_{<k}) = 0$. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Feldman Verifiable Secret Sharing (VSS) Soundness

To eliminate Byzantine node corruption or malicious share modification in transit, the dealer computes commitments in $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$:
$$C_i = g^{a_i} \pmod p \quad \text{for } i \in \{0, 1, \dots, k-1\} \quad (\text{with } a_0 = S)$$

Each share recipient or verifier tests:
$$g^{y_j} \stackrel{?}{\equiv} \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (C_i)^{x_j^i \pmod q} \pmod p$$

### Theorem 2 (Computational Soundness under Discrete Logarithm)
An adversary who attempts to forge a corrupted share $y_j' \ne y_j$ such that $g^{y_j'} \equiv \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (C_i)^{x_j^i} \pmod p$ must solve the Discrete Logarithm Problem in the subgroup $G_q$.

#### Proof:
Suppose an adversary finds $y_j' \ne y_j \pmod q$ satisfying:
$$g^{y_j'} \equiv \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (C_i)^{x_j^i} \pmod p$$
Dividing by the authentic verification equation $g^{y_j} \equiv \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (C_i)^{x_j^i} \pmod p$ yields:
$$g^{y_j' - y_j} \equiv 1 \pmod p$$
Since $g$ generates a subgroup of prime order $q$, $g^d \equiv 1 \pmod p$ if and only if $d \equiv 0 \pmod q$. Therefore:
$$y_j' - y_j \equiv 0 \pmod q \implies y_j' \equiv y_j \pmod q$$
Contradiction. Thus, any modified share is rejected with probability $1 - 2^{-255}$. $\blacksquare$

## 4. Lagrange Basis Polynomial Invariance & Reconstruction

Given any subset of $k$ valid shares $\{(x_j, y_j)\}_{j=1}^k$, the secret $S = f(0)$ is reconstructed using Lagrange basis polynomials:
$$L_j(0) = \prod_{m \ne j, m=1}^k \frac{x_m}{x_m - x_j} \pmod q$$
$$S = \sum_{j=1}^k y_j \cdot L_j(0) \pmod q$$

The modular inverse $(x_m - x_j)^{-1} \pmod q$ is computed via Fermat's Little Theorem:
$$(x_m - x_j)^{-1} \equiv (x_m - x_j)^{q-2} \pmod q$$
which executes in strict constant time, mitigating timing side-channel attacks.
